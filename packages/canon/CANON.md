# Canon: the canon checker

How the canon checker behaves now, and why. It is the canon of the package `@bigmistqke/canon`. Its terms and its claims are written in Alloy, and the statement of a claim, or of a term that defines a word, is said from its predicate by `canon generate`, so that the units can be checked against each other. [The canon skill](../../skills/canon/SKILL.md) describes the method.

### @term runner

> A runner is the test library whose calls the checker reads as tests: Vitest, Jest, Playwright, Node's test runner, or a library the setting `runners` describes. A function that a runner marker marks is a call of a runner too.

The setting `runners` lists the runners of a project, such as `runners: [vitest, playwright]`. A name picks a preset: `vitest`, `jest`, `playwright` or `node`. A map such as `{calls: [specify], modifiers: [only, skip], fails: [failing]}` describes a runner with no preset. A project that sets no `runners` has every preset.

A runner has three lists. Its calls start a test, such as `it` or `test`. Its modifiers keep a call a test, such as `only` in `it.only`. Its failure modifiers keep a call a test and mark it to fail, such as `fails` in `it.fails`.

A runner marker is a line `@runner` in the JSDoc of a function in a suite or a source, such as a helper that wraps `it`. The line `@runner fails` marks a function whose calls are expected failures.

## @axiom the-check-can-be-taken-at-its-word

> The author can act on what the checker reports: a clean check means the canon holds, and a finding means it does not.

A checker can act as an advisor, whose findings are hints the author weighs. It can also act as a judge, whose verdict the author acts on. The canon takes the judge. The canon stays current only because the check fails when it does not. An author who has to check the checker learns to ignore it, and then nothing keeps the canon current.

### @axiom a-canon-cannot-go-out-of-date-unnoticed

<!-- statement-axiom-a-canon-cannot-go-out-of-date-unnoticed:begin — generated by `canon generate`; edits are overwritten -->
> All of these hold:
> - for every [claim_1](#term-claim):
>
>   when claim_1 is [unguarded](#term-unguarded), [checker](#term-checker) reports a [finding](#term-finding) for claim_1
> - for every [tag_1](#term-tag):
>
>   when tag_1 [names](#term-name) no [unit](#term-unit), checker reports a finding for tag_1
> - for every [test_1](#term-test):
>
>   when test_1 is [adrift](#term-adrift), checker reports a finding for test_1
> - for every [pointer_1](#term-pointer):
>
>   when pointer_1 names no [target](#term-target), checker reports a finding for pointer_1
> - for every unit_1:
>
>   when unit_1 is not [used](#term-used) and is not a [conflict](#term-conflict), checker reports a finding for unit_1
> - for every unit_1:
>
>   when a [trailer](#term-trailer) [follows](#term-follow) unit_1, checker reports a finding for unit_1
> - for every [document_1](#term-document):
>
>   when document_1 [holds](#term-hold) a unit and holds no [index](#term-index), checker reports a finding for document_1
> - for every document_1:
>
>   when document_1 holds an index that does not [match](#term-match) document_1, checker reports a finding for document_1
> - for every claim_1:
>
>   when claim_1 names a [site](#term-site) that no [source](#term-source) holds, checker reports a finding for claim_1
> - for every unit_1:
>
>   when unit_1 [repeats](#term-repeat) a unit, checker reports a finding for unit_1.
<!-- statement-axiom-a-canon-cannot-go-out-of-date-unnoticed:end -->

This narrows taking the check at its word to a clean check: when the checker reports nothing, the canon has not gone out of date.

Documentation goes out of date because nothing checks it. A canon goes out of date unnoticed when a claim no test checks stops holding, or when a test stops saying which claim it checks. It goes out of date as well when a link no longer leads where it says. So every leaf claim is [tested](#term-tested), and every [test](#term-test) [checks](#term-check) a leaf through its [tags](#term-tag). A claim that is not is [unguarded](#term-unguarded), and a test that does not is [adrift](#term-adrift).

So too every [pointer](#term-pointer), [index](#term-index) and [site](#term-site) leads where it says, and every id names one unit.

```alloy
(all c: Claim | unguarded[c] implies c in Finding)
and (all g: Tag | (no u: Unit | g->u in Name.tag) implies g in Finding)
and (all t: Test | adrift[t] implies t in Finding)
and (all p: Pointer | (no x: Target | p->x in Name.pointer) implies p in Finding)
and (all u: Unit | (not used[u] and u not in Conflict) implies u in Finding)
and (all u: Unit | (some t: Trailer | t->u in Follow.rel) implies u in Finding)
and (all d: Document | ((some u: Unit | d->u in Hold.units) and (no i: Index | d->i in Hold.indexes)) implies d in Finding)
and (all d: Document | (some i: Index | d->i in Hold.indexes and i->d not in Match.rel) implies d in Finding)
and (all c: Claim | (some s: Site | c->s in Name.site and (no r: Source | r->s in Hold.symbols)) implies c in Finding)
and (all u: Unit | (some v: Unit | u->v in Repeat.rel) implies u in Finding)
```
