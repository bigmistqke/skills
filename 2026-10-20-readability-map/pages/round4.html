Keep
"The checker reports a finding for every unit that is unfounded" (C2) holds its subject for a weak reader better than "for every unit_1: when unit_1 is unfounded, checker reports a finding for unit_1". Dropping numbers (pattern A) helps on its own.
Avoid
"It", "the first", "the other" where two things of a kind are in play; "any" in a condition; "X is P when Q" for a rule that only goes one way, which reads as "exactly when".
4

Formulas that slipped: what the loop found in the canons

your judgement

Where a natural reading differs from the formula, sometimes the formula is what is off. Opus and Sonnet then audited all 209 units on their own, against each unit's name, what it refines and its rationale, and wrote the formula they think was meant. The analyzer confirmed every intended formula parses and differs.

s067 · a nested units block is its own

flagged by Opus and Sonnet

The direction of Sit.rel is swapped: it excludes blocks of the units that sit in u, its children, where the rationale is about the unit u sits in.

Formula now
… (no v: Unit | v->u in Sit.rel and v->f in Blocks.rel)
Formula meant
… (no v: Unit | u->v in Sit.rel and v->f in Blocks.rel)

s007 · used

flagged by Opus and Sonnet

A unit counts as used when another sits in it, but not when another derives from it, though the rationale counts whatever relies on it.

Formula now
(some v: Unit | v != u and v->u in Sit.rel) or …
Formula meant
(some v: Unit | v != u and cites[v, u]) or …

s091, s094, s125, s151 · a variable bound around a claim it only partly uses

flagged by Opus (s125, s151); every back-translation (s091, s094)

The run, or the scenario, is bound around the whole claim, so the part that does not mention it only holds when one exists. "A refused frontmatter fails the run" does not even require the report when there is no run.

Formula now
all r: Run, d: Document, f: Refused |
  d->f in Head.rel implies (d in Unreadable and r in Exits2)
Formula meant
(all d: Document, f: Refused | d->f in Head.rel implies d in Unreadable)
and (all r: Run, d: Document, f: Refused |
  d->f in Head.rel implies r in Exits2)

six statements · "another" with no u != v

flagged by every back-translation

"A unit is declared once", "units that share a word are asked about together", "a document is context for another" all allow a unit or document paired with itself. Harmless if the model makes the relation irreflexive; it does not, since the analyzer found such worlds.

Formula now
all u: Unit | (some v: Unit | u->v in Repeat.rel)
  implies u in Rotting
Formula meant
all u: Unit | (some v: Unit | v != u and u->v in Repeat.rel)
  implies u in Rotting

s102 · a run exits with the worst code of its plugins

flagged by Opus only

Codes 2 and 1 are covered; a run whose plugins all return 0 may still exit with 1 or 2, so s105, "a run with no finding succeeds", does not follow.

Formula now
… and ((some p | code1) and (no p | code2) implies r in Exits1)
Formula meant
… and ((no p | code1 or code2) implies r in Exits0)

s119 · a generated statement its formula would say again is current

flagged by Sonnet only

Its siblings for stale, unwritten and handwritten all require the unit to hold a formula; current does not.

Formula now
all u: Unit | (u in Generated and …) implies u in Current
Formula meant
all u: Unit | (u in Logical and u in Generated and …)
  implies u in Current

Sonnet also flagged s230 (lifetimes) for ordering sibling sub-owners, which Opus read as intended. Left out.

5

What the map suggests

  1. Collapse with guardrails. C1 and C2 are safe and help weak readers, with the rules of section 2: no "another" without !=, no variable moved past a part that does not use it, no relative clause that flips an argument, no "it" where two of a kind are in play.
  2. An intent check in canon-plugin-alloy. Two lints need no model: a variable bound around a claim but missing from part of it, and two variables of one signature with no != in a relation the model does not make irreflexive. The loop above, run on demand, catches the rest.
  3. The six slips of section 4 are yours to judge: fix the formula, or say why it is meant.
