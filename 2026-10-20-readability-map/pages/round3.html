U

until and since: a ladder down from the ideal

your call

The ideal sentence drops three things the formula says. For until: the owner is disposed at some point, the registration need not hold at that moment, and if the disposal is now, nothing else is required. Each rung puts back what the ideal drops, in as few words as it can.

This time the probe asked no Alloy. Eight Haiku readers each saw one wording per formula, with six timelines, and said whether the sentence holds on each one. The timelines test the edges: the end never comes, it comes now, the moment of the end itself, after the end, the first or last of several ends, and a gap. Each count below is right answers out of answers, over three formulas and two readers per wording. "Hard" is the readers' rating from 1 to 5.

Until
The ideal fails where the disposal never comes: 1 of 6 right. That loss is real, not a reading quirk. R2 is one sentence and exactly as accurate as the two-part form (34 of 36 each), and rated easier; R1 is close behind (32). Their remaining mistakes are on one edge: when the disposal is now, readers still want the registration now. R2 says that edge out loud.
Since
"Ever since" reads as including the moment of the disposal: R1 gets 0 of 6 on the disposal-now edge. So "ever since" is out. R2 keeps the explicit "after the last such moment, up to and including now" on one line, and scores close to the two-part form (30 against 32 of 36).
Lean
Use R2 for both, where each side is one clause. Keep the two parts where a side is a list or holds a time phrase of its own. The strict judges check R2 before it ships.
